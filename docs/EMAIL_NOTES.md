# Facts for writing to Prof. Kavraki
These are facts to use, not a draft. Each one traces to `results/summary.json` or `docs/`. Text in [brackets] waits for the UR5 / v1-paper-scene rerun.

1. **Setup.** We compared VAMP (`27cb9b6`), cuRobo v1 (the version our paper used) and the current cuRoboV2, all on a single Jetson Orin NX 16 GB. VAMP ran on one Cortex-A78AE core with NEON, and both cuRobo versions on the Orin NX's GPU. All three checked the same sphere models against the same obstacles, and their collision checkers agreed on all 16,000 random configurations we tested.
2. **Panda results.** On VAMP's MotionBenchMaker Panda problems, VAMP solved 699/699 with a median of 1.76 ms per query (wall clock, including Python). cuRobo v1 solved 638/699 with a median of 849 ms, about 480x slower. On a 69-problem subset, cuRoboV2 solved 55 with a median of 6.55 s.
3. **Paper scenes.** We used cricket to generate VAMP models for the two robots in our paper: the UR5e, and the UR5e on a 7th-axis rail. On the paper's pick-and-place scenes, VAMP's median was 0.55–0.97 ms per query and cuRoboV2's was 0.38–1.06 s [cuRobo v1: pending].
4. **Collision check.** We re-checked every returned trajectory with VAMP's checker. Some of cuRoboV2's reported successes cut through an obstacle between two waypoints: 4/59 on Panda, 16/100 on the rail-with-pillars scene and 4/102 on the UR5e-with-pillars scene. None of cuRobo v1's 638 Panda successes did.
5. **Availability.** The code, the problem sets and the per-query results are in one repository, so her group can reproduce or extend the comparison (for example, to an x86 CPU or a desktop GPU).
